Consolidation plan · Béa v8.7.2

Fewer, Once, Same

Béa does not read as homemade because it is ugly. The typography, the oklch palette and the motion work are better than most funded apps. It reads as homemade because it does eighteen things in a dozen visual dialects and narrates itself while doing them.

Fewer things, said once, drawn the same way each time. Three workstreams, in that order of leverage. Two are mechanical and I can do them. The first is yours.

The spec that proposed them warned about exactly this. Section is the counter-example that proves the approach works when the migration actually happens.

PART 1

Fewer things

The tell of a solo-plus-AI app is not bad features. It is breadth without depth — many things, each about 80% finished. In a single session we found trips that would not open, pasted links that never resolved, and a Montreal restaurant saved in Slovakia. Those bugs existed because there is more surface than one person can keep working. They are symptoms, not coincidences.

This part is yours, not mine. I can measure the surface and argue about cohesion; I cannot tell you what Béa is. What follows is a framework and my read of it — the classification is a proposal to argue with, not a recommendation to execute.

The test

Béa's own mission is the arbiter: remembers your travel life so Future You doesn't miss what matters. The loop that delivers it is save → surface → plan → remember. For each feature, one question: if this vanished tonight, does the loop still close?

Feature Role in the loop Read
Recs vault The save. Nothing works without it. Core
Near The surface. "Past You left a breadcrumb" is the moment that justifies the app. Core
Trips The plan. Where saved things become an itinerary. Core
World / globe The remember, and the only thing no competitor has. Core
Trip stops & timeline Trips is not a feature without them. Core
Preferences Feeds the planner. Invisible but load-bearing. Core
City memories Overlaps the globe and the story page. Three views of one idea. Merge
Travel story A playback of what the globe already shows. Merge
Photo import A pipeline, not a destination. Belongs inside memories. Merge
Calendar Re-presents trip dates you can already see on the trip. Merge
Packing lists Real utility, but a to-do list with a hat on. Merge with trip to-dos. Merge
Trip budget Optional per trip already. Keep, but stop it being a section everyone sees. Merge
Business expenses Receipts and billable flags. This is a different product wearing Béa's clothes. Cut

Cut does not mean delete

Demote. Keep the data and the code; remove the route, the tab entry and the guide steps. The heatmap becomes a toggle on the globe rather than a section. Nobody loses anything; the app gets a screen shorter.

Merge. Memories, Story and Photos are three routes over one dataset — your photographs, by place, over time. That is one screen with three views, not three screens. Same for Packing and To-dos: both are "things to tick off before you go."

Archive. Expenses is ~480 lines and a database table serving a use case the mission does not mention. Branch it, remove it from the build, keep the migration. If someone asks, it comes back in a day.

Net effect

21 routes → roughly 12. Six tabs stay six. The app stops being a suite and starts being a product. Every hour not spent maintaining the other nine is an hour spent finishing these.

PART 2

Said once

86 guide steps and 4,311 words explaining the interface. Established apps say almost nothing, because the design carries the meaning. Heavy explanation is a symptom rather than a feature: the copy is there because a screen did not communicate on its own.

The rule

A guide step is a bug report filed against the screen it describes.

Every step you delete by making the screen clearer is worth more than the step. Every step you keep is a screen admitting it failed. Read all 86 that way and most of them argue for a change to the interface instead.

Screen Steps Words What the length is telling you
Recommendation vault 8 245 The 1,062-line route. The biggest screen needs the most explaining — that is the finding.
Inside a trip 7 258 Four icon buttons with no labels. The guide is the labels.
You / profile 8 200 A settings page needing 200 words is a settings page with too much in it.
World 6 209 Globe + stats + heatmap + filters. Three features explaining themselves at once.
Home 6 117 Six steps for a landing screen means it is not a landing screen.
Preferences 6 80 Short steps — these are genuine labels, and should become labels.

What to do with each

Becomes a label. Most of them. "The checklist icon opens the trip's errands" is a title attribute that should be visible text under the icon. Label the four trip icons and seven steps evaporate.

Becomes an empty state. "Add every city — and any stopover along the way" belongs where the list is empty, not in a tour someone takes once.

Becomes nothing. Anything explaining a feature that Part 1 removes.

Stays. The genuinely non-obvious: that recs are private, that documents are encrypted on-device, that Béa does not book anything. Trust claims earn their words. Target around 25 steps, down from 86.

Do not touch the voice. bea-voice.ts is the app's personality and it is good. The cut is aimed at instruction, not character. "Past You left a breadcrumb" stays. "Tap the sparkle to open the planner" goes and becomes a labelled button.
PART 3

Drawn the same way

Mechanical, low-risk, entirely mine to do. This is the part that makes the app look designed rather than assembled, and it is already half-specified — the primitives exist and almost nothing uses them.

Sequence, worst offenders first

Wave Files Surfaces Why here
1 TripDetail, profile, recommendations ~30 The three screens people spend real time in.
2 index, world, opportunities, trips ~22 The remaining tabs. After this, every tab is consistent.
3 TimelineEntryForm, ItineraryImport, PackingLists, ShareRecos ~20 Shared components — one fix lands on several screens.
4 Whatever survives Part 1 ~35 Deliberately last. Do not migrate a screen you are about to delete.

What each wave actually does

Replace every ad-hoc surface with one of four primitives, and nothing else:

  • PageHeader — already on every screen via AppShell. Done.
  • Section — 40 uses. Extend to every grouped block.
  • ContentCard — 4 uses. This is the wave's real work.
  • Sheet — does not exist yet. Four screens hand-roll a fixed-inset scrim dialog. It is the fifth primitive and the plan should admit that up front rather than discovering it in wave 3.

Then lock the door

Consistency decays unless regression is impossible. The motion tokens already have a lint rule rejecting arbitrary durations; surfaces get the same treatment, so the next hand-rolled card fails CI rather than shipping.

PART 4

Free wins, today

Remove the version badge. v8.7.2 sits beside the logo on every screen. No consumer app does this; it reads as a dev build, and it is in the corner of every screenshot you will ever show anyone. One line.

Label the four trip icons. Deletes several guide steps as a side effect, and makes the trip page legible without a tour.

Build the trip map. Not polish — an absence. Every itinerary item and every stop already stores lat/lon and none of it is ever drawn. A travel app where your trip is not on a map reads as unfinished no matter what else is true, and it needs no new data, no migration and no API.

PART 5

Order, and how you will know

Order Work Whose Reversible
1 Free wins — badge, icon labels Mine Trivially
2 Migration waves 1–3, then the lint rule Mine Yes, per wave
3 Trip map Mine Additive
4 Decide the cut list Yours —
5 Demote / merge / archive Mine, once decided Yes — branches, not deletions
6 Copy cut, guided by what survives Mine Yes
7 Migration wave 4 Mine Yes

Parts 2 and 3 are sequenced after the cut decision on purpose, for the same reason wave 4 is last: polishing and de-narrating a screen you are about to remove is the most wasteful work available. The free wins and waves 1–3 do not depend on the decision, so they can start now.

Done when

  • Raw card-soft and bg-elevated fail lint outside the primitives.
  • Guide steps under 30, and every survivor is a trust claim rather than an instruction.
  • No screen opens with anything but PageHeader.
  • Every route reachable from a tab in two taps or fewer.
  • A trip shows on a map.
  • Nothing in the header identifies the build.
The measurement that actually matters is still missing. None of this is observable today, because the app collects nothing — item 08 of the build spec, still unbuilt. Five people, twenty minutes each, before and after, remains cheaper and more conclusive than every metric in this document.
